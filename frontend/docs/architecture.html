Architectures used in my project

These are not interchangeable names. Each model changes what structural information is preserved and what gets blurred away.

GCN

GCN performs normalized neighborhood aggregation, which makes it stable and simple. The downside is that normalization can suppress raw count signals. For count-like tasks, that can become a limitation.

GraphSAGE

In this project, SAGE uses aggr="add", which tends to preserve count information better than mean aggregation. That aligns with its strong degree performance in my saved runs.

aggr="add" means neighbor messages are summed, not averaged.

GIN

GIN combines sum aggregation with an MLP and learnable self-weight, aiming at strong structural discrimination within the 1-WL family. It is often a robust choice when structural distinctions matter.

1-WL refers to the one-dimensional Weisfeiler-Lehman graph refinement test, a standard notion of graph distinguishability.

Loopy

Loopy injects r-neighborhood path information and is intentionally biased toward cycle-sensitive structure. In my experiments, this is exactly where it shines: minimum-cycle prediction.

r-neighborhood here means paths/structures around a node up to radius r, giving the model richer cycle context than plain one-hop message passing.
